**Assignment:** Client-Based Digital Campaign Plan (semester-long project)
**Submitted:** November 20, 2024
**Client:** Blacksburg Housing Equity Coalition (where I had just started as a
volunteer content writer)
**Audience:** Course instructor, client contact, and class critique panel

---

## Campaign brief

The coalition needed a four-week social content plan to promote a plain-language guide
to housing assistance programs ahead of the county's application deadline. My plan
covered platform selection, a content calendar, sample posts, and a short measurement
plan.

**Platform decision:** I recommended Facebook over Instagram as the primary platform,
against my initial instinct, after looking at the coalition's actual audience data —
older residents and non-English-dominant households were disproportionately reached
through Facebook groups run by local churches and civic associations. This was the
first time a class project forced me to design for a documented audience instead of an
assumed one.

**Sample post (Week 2, English/Spanish pair):**

> Need help applying for housing assistance before the December 1 deadline? Our free
> guide walks you through it step by step — no jargon, no wrong answers. Link in bio,
> or stop by our Wednesday office hours at the community center.
>
> ¿Necesita ayuda para solicitar asistencia de vivienda antes del 1 de diciembre?
> Nuestra guía gratuita lo explica paso a paso. Enlace en la biografía, o visítenos los
> miércoles en el centro comunitario.

**Measurement plan:** link clicks to the guide, office-hours attendance during the
campaign window, and — since the coalition explicitly did not want vanity metrics —
a follow-up count of completed applications where the client was able to track them.

---

**Reflection** *(added for portfolio)*

This was the first project where "know your audience" stopped being a slogan and
became a set of decisions I had to defend to a real client who would use my work. The
platform-selection reversal is the part I'm proudest of: my first draft optimized for
what looked good in class, and the client's actual usage data made me redo it. That
habit — check what you assume against what you can verify — is the same instinct I now
apply to auditing AI-drafted content for algorithmic audience assumptions in CMST 4714.
