## Situation analysis (my section, condensed)

Micah's Backpack had a volunteer retention problem: sign-ups were strong after each
recruitment push, but fewer than a third of new volunteers returned for a second shift.
Our formative research — a 12-question survey (n=64) plus four volunteer interviews I
conducted — found the core issue was not the work itself but the onboarding experience:
new volunteers were handed a task list with no explanation of impact, and left the first
shift unsure whether what they had done mattered.

## Message strategy (my section, condensed)

We recommended repositioning the volunteer pitch from *"help us pack food"* to *"you
are the last link between a warehouse and a kid's Friday backpack"* — grounding every
volunteer communication in the specific chain of impact from task to recipient, and
building a short first-shift orientation script that named that chain explicitly before
any task assignment.

**Sample orientation line (drafted by me, tested with two pilot volunteer groups):**

> Everything on this table goes home with a kid this Friday. You're not just packing
> bags — you're the reason a kid gets to eat on the weekend without their family having
> to ask. Let's get started.

## Outcome

The client adopted the revised onboarding script for the fall 2026 semester; our team
does not have return-rate data yet, since that would only be measurable a full
semester after implementation.

**Reflection** *(added for portfolio)*

This was my first project where the writing itself was a small part of the work, and
the research design was the part that actually mattered — you cannot write a message
strategy you can defend to an executive director without first finding out why the
current message isn't landing. That research-before-writing discipline is exactly what
I now bring to diagnosing AI-generated communication failures in CMST 4714: before I
can explain why an automated message failed, I have to establish, evidence in hand,
who the actual audience was and what they needed.
